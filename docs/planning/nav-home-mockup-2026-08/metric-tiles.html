The tile system — and what it displaces
1

Hero tile, full-bleed chart. The Reports-card anatomy — tracked kicker, bold value, quiet sub-line, accent ↗ — scaled up, with the 14-day score curve behind the numeral. Two hard rules keep the number winning: the curve may not enter the text zone (its left half rides low; the climb happens right of the copy), and the text zone carries a surface-colour veil. Same 74 · “6 pts above your 30-day baseline” the current strip shows.

2

Deviation copy is the sub-line, valence is the colour. “12% below your 30-day baseline” tints strain because fewer steps strains the goal — not because “below” maps to a colour; a resting-HR tile 5% above baseline would tint the same strain amber. Sleep sits on its baseline, so it stays neutral grey. Dot always paired with the label (WCAG 1.4.1).

3

Three sizes, one grammar. Small tiles keep kicker + value + a compressed spark or bar strip; deviation copy shortens to “on baseline” with the full sentence one tap away. Sizing is recommendation R5 from the benchmark doc — the one thing My Space can’t do today.

4

Page dots, not a wall. Two pages max by default. Page 1 is the daily glance; page 2 is opt-in depth (section below).

5

Pencil = the existing customiser, extended. This is not a new surface — it opens the My Space editor (home_my_space_screen.dart), which already does reorder, show/hide and presets; tiles add resize and the background chart.

6

What the grid displaces: the hero-score strip, and only that. The old 4-cell metrics block (Today Score / Steps / Sleep / Ready) is fully absorbed — every number it showed is a tile above — so it is removed, not duplicated. The score/streak deck stays: its content (goals ring, login streak, volume/time/PRs) is gamification, not health metrics, and nothing in the grid covers it. The honest cost is visible here: Home gets ~110px longer than the current build, because the tile grid is taller than the strip it replaces. Follow-up option, not in this proposal: offer Streak / Volume / PRs as addable tiles and let users who adopt them hide the deck.

=

Everything else is byte-identical to the current Home. Banner → tiles → coach → deck → browse → workout hero → quick actions → fuel → reports → timeline (still last). No section re-sequenced, no content invented; the surrounding markup is lifted verbatim from the round-3 After frame.

Chart in the background
The design problem, solved explicitly: the same Steps tile at three treatments. The number must always win, and the deviation text must never sit on a steep part of the curve.
A — hero tile onlyFull-bleed, low opacity
Steps 6,412 12% below your 30-day baseline
Maximum drama, conditional legibility. Works when the numeral is huge (the 56px hero) and the left third is veiled. At a medium tile’s 26px value the curve competes with the type — and because data shape changes daily, some mornings the steepest segment lands exactly under the deviation line. The veil is a crutch; on the hero it’s a fair one, on smaller tiles it dims the very chart the treatment exists to show.
B — recommended · M + S defaultLower-third band
Steps 6,412 12% below your 30-day baseline
Structurally clean, whatever the data does. Kicker, value and deviation live in the upper zone; the chart owns the lower third. No veil needed, full-strength stroke, and the “text on a steep slope” failure is impossible by construction — the two zones never overlap. Cost: amplitude is compressed, so the chart reads as trend-direction, not precise shape. For a glance tile that is the right trade.
C — rejectedBaseline-anchored deviation band
Steps 6,412 12% below your 30-day baseline
Double-encodes, then misleads. Shading the gap between the curve and the dashed baseline says what the deviation copy already says — and the fill reads as a meter: on a quiet week the band is visually silent (tile looks “empty”), on a volatile week it floods the tile and the value drowns. It also breaks the family grammar: everywhere else in the doc the area gradient hangs below the line, not between line and reference.
The ruling

A for the hero, B for everything else, C dropped. One grammar per size, no per-tile options — treatment is a property of the size, so resizing a tile in edit mode also re-homes its chart. Both keep the unified stroke 1.6, endpoint dot r=2, and the dashed 30-day baseline wherever a deviation is claimed.

The paging rule Google never wrote down
1

Page 1 = what you check daily. Score, movement, sleep, the three glanceables. Page 2 = opt-in depth — HRV, resting HR, protein — metrics a user deliberately placed there. A metric only exists on a page because someone put it there; nothing auto-fills page 2, so it can never rot into a dumping ground.

2

The peek is the tutorial. Page 2’s tiles bleed ~28px past the right edge at rest — the paging model is discoverable without onboarding copy. Google’s secondary pages were reachable “by swiping” with no visual hint they existed.

3

Dots under the grid, not under the screen. The pager owns only the tile section; coach, workout and everything below stay fixed while pages swipe above them.

=

No “Expanded view” mode. Google bolted on a separate expanded-view setting to show more metrics without swiping. Here that need is answered by the same two primitives users already have: resize a tile up, or place it on page 2.

What Google shipped · May 2026

Their mistakes, named

Google Health’s focus tiles are the model — snapshots of single data points, a big item beside smaller tiles, secondary pages by swipe. The execution is where they were punished, in reviews and in point-release patch notes. Each failure below maps to a specific decision in this mockup.

No reorder at launch
Google — shipped

The recommended workaround was to delete every default tile and re-add them one by one in the order you wanted. Drag-to-reorder arrived only in later point releases — long-press-drag on Android, drag-and-drop in iOS 5.03.

This design — day one

Long-press-drag is in the first build, with a live drop placeholder (Section 2). It is the core interaction of edit mode, not a fast-follow. The Add sheet is for adding — never the ordering tool.

Oversized tiles, dead space
Google — shipped

Reviewers: “excessive white space”, “oversized tiles”, “a huge block of empty space” underneath — a layout that “reads unfinished rather than intentional”.

This design — density by size

Three tile sizes on a 6-column grid pack the same 390pt width Google left hollow — and tiles are a section of Home, not a page. Coach, workout hero and reports continue directly beneath; there is no canvas to leave empty.

Enable, then drag
Google — shipped

To prioritise sleep you had to enable the feature separately, then manually drag it into place — customisation reviewers called counterintuitive.

This design — one gesture

Adding a metric from the Add sheet is enabling it — placed, sized and live in one act. Removal is symmetric: one tap hides the tile, history intact, re-addable any time. Matches My Space’s existing show/hide semantics.

“Expanded view” as a separate mode
Google — shipped

More metrics without swiping lived behind a pencil-menu “Expanded view” option — a third layout state alongside the default and the swipe pages.

This design — two primitives, no modes

Want a metric bigger? Resize it (S/M/L). Want more metrics? Place them on page 2. Every layout a user can reach is expressed by the same two primitives — there is no third mode to discover, document, or forget about.

Where Zealova starts ahead. My Space (home_my_space_screen.dart) already ships drag-reorder, show/hide and presets — the exact capabilities Google launched without. Tiles extend that editor with the two missing pieces: per-tile resize (benchmark recommendation R5) and the background chart. This is an upgrade to an existing, proven surface, not a new customisation system.

The same real Home, Health not connected. Sensor tiles degrade to labelled empty states — never a blank box, never a fabricated number. Manual metrics stay live.

Degradation rules
1

Labelled empty state, stable layout. A source-less tile keeps its exact size and grammar — kicker, an em-dash where the value would be, and a dashed capsule naming the reason. No chart is drawn at all: a flat fake line would be a fabricated shape. This is the per-signal no_data capsule behaviour vitals_detail_screen.dart already ships, carried into tiles.

2

The hero never goes dark. Today Score is computed from in-app workouts and logs, so the top of Home is alive on a fresh install — and its sub-line says so instead of claiming a baseline that doesn’t exist yet (no deviation line until 7+ days of history).

3

Manual metrics don’t degrade. Water and Weight are logged in-app; their tiles stay fully live with charts. The empty state is per-source, never a page-wide takeover.

4

One recovery path, stated honestly. The Connect card sits directly under the grid and promises only what connecting does. Never a magic number: same principle as the nullable nutrition targets — if a value isn’t real, no surface renders one.

=

The rest of Home is the real page, honest about the same gap. Coach card, deck, workout hero, fuel, reports all render as normal — and the timeline has no “Woke up” row, because sleep has no source either. The hero score also drops its dashed baseline: no deviation is claimed, so no reference line is drawn.

Why this matters

A grid that fabricates zeros reads as “you did nothing”; a grid that goes blank reads as broken. Labelled per-tile degradation is the only state that reads as honest — and it converts: the dark tiles themselves are the pitch for connecting Health.

System rules
The contract every tile obeys, wherever it lands.
Tile grammar — non-negotiables
✦
Reports-card DNA. Tracked uppercase kicker · bold value · quiet sub-line · accent ↗ bottom-right. Same 14px radius, same border, same light-mode shadow ladder as every card in the family.
✦
Chart treatment is a property of size. Large → full-bleed (lower 60% + veil). Medium/small → lower-third band. Users pick sizes, never chart styles.
✦
Unified chart chrome. Stroke 1.6, endpoint dot r=2, dashed 30-day baseline wherever a deviation is claimed. Charts speak accent; per-series hues (macros, sleep stages) survive only inside detail views.
✦
Valence, not direction. supports · neutral · strains. Resting HR above baseline tints strain; sleep above baseline tints support. Every tint is glued to its text label — colour never carries meaning alone.
✦
Deviation copy is the voice. “12% below your 30-day baseline” on M/L tiles; small tiles shorten to “above/below baseline” with the full sentence on tap-through. Bare numbers are never presented as judgement.
✦
One accent, twelve values. Tile chrome runs entirely on the user’s chosen accent + monochrome — including monochrome. No tile design may depend on a specific hue.
✦
No fabricated data, ever. Missing source → labelled capsule. Insufficient history → value without a deviation claim. The chart is drawn from real points or not at all.
Build surface
→
Extends, not replaces. Edit mode is the existing My Space editor (home_my_space_screen.dart) plus resize; tiles replace the Home metrics strip only. Presets keep working — a preset now also carries sizes and page assignment.
→
Instant paint. Tiles render from the disk cache first like every other Home section; charts hydrate in place. No skeleton grid — collapse-not-skeleton applies per tile.

Round 4 · device findings

One band, one owner,
one diameter

Three circles at three diameters in one 44 pt band is the defect the founder photographed. The cluster is not a place where floats accumulate — it is a closed two-slot row with a single owner, and every circular float in the app derives its size and position from the same two tokens. The bubble-with-a-badge coach glyph goes too: a standalone sparkle is the settled convention for “AI is the whole point of this control”, and it is what this mockup drew in the first place.
The coach glyph
A sparkle with a bubble around it says “messaging, which happens to have AI”. A standalone sparkle says “this control IS the AI”. That is the whole difference between what shipped and what the founder expected.
Shippedbubble + badge
“is that a camera?”

18 pt, accent

Why the sparkle, settled

Google standardised the sparkle across Material and Gemini in 2023; Copilot, OpenAI and Adobe all use it. The convention distinguishes the two forms precisely: a sparkle attached to something means “AI-enhance this thing”; a standalone sparkle means “AI is this control’s whole job”. This button’s whole job is the AI coach.

Where the clearance comes from

Not from more scroll padding — the trailing 180 pt sliver already exists and cannot help, because on a page taller than the viewport something is always under the band at rest. It comes from the scrim that stops one pixel too soon. The nav’s fade box is 56 + inset + kMainNavFadeHeight(36) tall, so its top edge lands at inset + 92 — exactly the FAB’s bottom edge. The whole 44 pt button and its 24 px shadow sit above the scrim, on raw fully-opaque content. Raise the fade to 102 so the scrim starts 20 pt above the band, and delete the one CTA card that landed inside it.

What the third circle probably was

A static trace of every mount reachable from main_shell and app.dart found no third float. Two candidates fit an orange circle at a third diameter and both get normalised by the same sweep:

The through-line for all four defects is the same one: a control that advertises something the code cannot do. Six capsules advertising one action, two dots advertising a page that is not mounted, a message glyph advertising a chat, and three diameters advertising three different kinds of button. Each fix deletes the advertisement, not the feature.
