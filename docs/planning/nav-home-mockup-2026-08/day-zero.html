The score-tile rule

The Today Score is an execution score — "how much of today's plan have you done". By construction it reads 0 every morning of every account's life. The 9:46 AM zero was never an onboarding bug; day 0 is just the first time you see it without yesterday's 84 in your memory.

So the rule is the one the codebase already committed to, applied to the one tile that was exempted from it: a tile mounts only when its source can produce a number. Before any contributor has real progress today, the score's source cannot — so it does not mount. It takes its stored slot the moment there is a point to show, which is the existing read-time filtering behaviour, not a new mechanism.

And when it does mount, it does not render a bare numeral above a void: the segmented capacity track fills the zone the undrawable sparkline was reserving, with segment widths set by the score's own renormalised weights. That also makes the afternoon tile better than today's, because you watch the bar close.

Rejected on the way here: leading the morning with Anton 100 and PTS OPEN. Arithmetically the same fact read from the other side, but a giant 100 under a kicker reading TODAY SCORE reads as a perfect day at a glance.

Parked — the Health moment

Documented, not scheduled. Nothing in the build below depends on it.

The current connect card describes a chore. The truth is more interesting: authorising Health grants access to past samples — hasRecentSamples(days: 30) already depends on this, which is why capability resolves instantly instead of after a waiting period. The card could sell the backfill ("Your phone already has the data — connecting reads your last 30 days back"), print a scope manifest derived from the requested-types constant, and stage the arrival: newly-capable tiles mounting ~90 ms apart, sparklines drawing from real history, each with a caption counted from actual sample days. metric_capability_provider.dart already promises an arriving tile is "announced rather than discovered mid-scroll" — that would build the announcement the comment owes.

What shipped

Built 2026-08-16. flutter analyze clean on every touched file; 4,052 tests pass, including a new day-zero widget test that fails if the score tile ever renders its zero on a fresh account again.

Three things landed differently from the drawings above, all deliberate:

1. Water and Weight keep their slots. They are logged in-app and need no device, so they were never candidates for substitution — only the four sensor-backed slots were handed to plan tiles. Maintenance and Week are catalog entries you can add from the pencil rather than defaults. 2. Daily fuel shows the target, not the intake — the Nourish and Protein tiles already render consumption, and two tiles counting the same calories is worse than one. 3. When the score returns it is a seventh tile, taking its own large slot above Next session, which stays where it is. Two large tiles is heavier than the drawing; both are draggable and resizable, so it is a starting arrangement rather than a fixed one.

One more rule earned its way in during the build. The score's mount gate cannot ask the history "have you ever scored" — ScoreHistoryNotifier keeps 90 days, so a user who scored in March and nothing since would answer no and lose a tile they had been reading. The first point is now a one-way persisted fact on the capability record, so the tile disappears exactly once in an account's life.

Nothing in this document renders a number the user did not type, log, or have computed from their own inputs. No skeletons, no sample data, no "coming soon", and _defaultOrder is untouched — every change lives inside the section that already occupies this slot.
