The evidence — convention + Material 3

The app already answered this

The Quick Log pill is not fixed-width: quickLogFabExpandedProvider (12 px hysteresis, main_shell.dart:683) collapses it to an icon circle the instant the tab scrolls, re-expands at the top, and hides it on the Coach tab. The bottom-right band breathes horizontally — exactly what breaks a coach control beside a fixed Quick Log or fused to it, and exactly the state D shares.

What ships today. One user-picked accent + the monochrome text ladder. Every orange element carries the same meaning — “interactive / yours” — and the Health sub-nav inherits the same accent for its active chip. Nothing competes with the data.
The Zealova-specific constraint — 12 user accents

Colour identity in this app is not fixed — the user picks it. The AccentColor enum (lib/core/theme/accent_color_provider.dart) ships twelve options; orange is only the default. Any fixed per-domain palette has to survive all twelve. Neither Samsung nor Oura has this constraint — it is unique to Zealova, and it decides the question (proof below).

monochrome cyan purple orange · default green blue red pink teal indigo amber lime
What one accent buys

In today’s system colour is a reserved channel: if something is coloured, it is interactive, active, or yours — a rule learned once that holds on every screen. Note the first enum value: monochrome — an “accent” that is no colour at all. Users who pick it get a fully legible app, which is only possible because no meaning anywhere rides on hue. Option 2 would break monochrome outright; Option 3 survives it, because every state tint carries its own words.

The Samsung path, rendered fairly. Training blue, Fuel green, Sleep purple, Recovery teal, Body pink — one hue per domain, applied consistently. It photographs well. The evidence here and the collision proof below are what kill it — not taste.
The evidence — Samsung Health 2026

Samsung added categorical colour with no learnable rule: purple = calories and sleep, blue = workouts, orange = stress. It was miss #1 in every review:

“colour for colour’s sake” · “no logical connection to the data” · “one of the ugliest things I have ever seen in an app” · “awful pukey yellow”

Accessibility — colour-only encoding fails

WCAG 1.4.1: colour must never be the sole means of conveying information — and domain hues on nav chips and metric values are exactly that: colour-as-category with no paired text. ~8% of men are colour-blind. An audit of 200 data dashboards found 94% had a critical accessibility issue, most commonly colour-only encoding. This palette walks straight into the most common failure in the category.

Even the disciplined version above inherits the structural failures: five hues on metric chrome means the accent stops meaning anything — and unlike Samsung, Zealova’s accent isn’t even a constant. See the collision proof below.

The call — Option 3

Accent unchanged for identity and actions; colour is added only where it encodes state — support or strain against your own baseline. Because the deviation copy (“12% below your 30-day baseline”) always ships glued to the tint, the text IS the encoding and the colour is redundant reinforcement — WCAG 1.4.1 is satisfied by construction, not by audit.

The counter-example — Oura 2026

The most respected design in the category introduced a “unified semantic colour system” where “dynamic colour cues direct attention to what matters most” and help members instantly understand how their habits and environments “support or strain” their health. Colour encoding state, not category. (Oura’s redesign also introduced a My Health tab — after Samsung’s Vitals pillar and Google’s Health tab, a third independent data point that health deserves its own destination; it reinforces the Health-tab frame above.)

Honest caveat: a green-accent user still meets a green “above baseline”. The ramp shrinks the collision surface from five hues on nav-and-metric chrome to two label-glued text tints — the collision degrades to harmless; it doesn’t vanish.

Dark mode — desaturate 20–40%

Saturated colour visually vibrates on dark surfaces; Material’s guidance is to desaturate roughly 20–40% for dark themes — and Zealova is dark-first, so a saturated multi-hue palette is its worst case. The ramp swaps per theme (--st-below / --st-above): flip this page to Dark and the crop above is already running the desaturated pair. Accent cyan vs Recovery teal — one perceptual family — with Training blue sitting next to both. The accent isn’t just colliding; it’s drowned — a sixth hue in a five-hue system, and the only one that means “you”.
A fixed domain palette cannot coexist with 12 user-selectable accents. Nine of the twelve shipped accents — green, lime, blue, indigo, cyan, teal, purple, pink, red — sit within collision range of at least one of the five domain hues. Samsung and Oura tune their palettes against one constant brand colour; Zealova’s “brand colour” is a user setting with twelve values. Option 2 doesn’t fail on taste — it fails structurally, and no palette re-pick fixes it without deleting the accent feature.
What stays categorical. Zealova already ships palettes that are earned and code-allowlisted, and this recommendation touches none of them: macro colours (protein purple · carbs cyan · fat orange), cycle-phase colours, social tab identity colours, and workout-type colours. Those are data series that genuinely need telling apart. The rule proposed here governs navigation and metric chrome only: chrome speaks accent + monochrome; state speaks the ramp; data series keep their own vocabularies.
